Winter break reading plan for kindergarteners: 10 minutes a day

A good winter break reading plan for a kindergartener is short and steady: about 10 minutes a day of your child reading or playing with sounds, plus books you read aloud to them. Reread a book they can already read (or practice a few letter sounds), play one quick sound game, and write one real thing, like a list or a card. Below is a two-week schedule of simple reading activities for winter break that fit around holidays and travel, plus a free printable reading bingo card and reading log.

Why is 10 minutes a day enough over winter break?

About 10 minutes a day is enough for most kindergarteners over winter break. Winter break is usually about two weeks, and five- and six-year-olds learn best in short, happy bursts. Ten minutes is easy to fit in, even on travel days and lazy pajama mornings. You don't need to push ahead. The point is to keep this fall's sounds and words fresh, so January feels like picking up where they left off.

Count your read-aloud time separately. Reading picture books aloud to your child, at any length, is wonderful on its own. The 10 minutes is the part where your child does the reading or the sound work.

What can most kindergarteners read by winter break?

By December, many kindergarteners know most letter sounds and are starting to sound out short words like cat, sun and bed. Some are already reading short books alone, and some are still learning their letter sounds. All of that is common in the middle of kindergarten.

So start where your child is right now. Not sure what they're working on? The take-home books and word lists from school are a good clue. If you have worries, jot them down and ask the teacher in January.

The daily 10-minute routine

  1. Read or reread (4 minutes). Your child reads a book or page they've read before. Rereading builds smooth, confident reading, so reach for an easy book. School books, a decodable book at their level (one that uses only sounds they know, plus a few practiced tricky words), or their own writing all count.
  2. Sound game (3 minutes). One quick game out loud, like the ones below. No paper needed.
  3. Write one real thing (3 minutes). One to three words is plenty, like a short list or a name on a card. Spelling the way it sounds is fine: "milk, egz, jam" shows they're hearing the sounds. If writing is still hard, your child can say the word, write the first letter, and you write the rest.

Still learning letter sounds? Use the first 4 minutes to point to four or five lowercase letters and say their sounds together, or hunt for one letter on a picture-book page and say its sound.

When your child gets stuck on a word: point under the letters from left to right and say "sound it out with me." If it's a word they can't sound out yet, like the or said, just tell them and keep going. Try not to say "guess from the picture."

Stop while it's still fun, even if you planned more. On a tired day, one part is plenty. If they're into it, keep going.

Quick winter break reading activities (no supplies needed)

Play these in the car, while you cook, or in line at the store.

In these games, letters between slashes mean the sound, not the letter name: /s/ means "sss," not "ess," and /m/ means "mmm." Say quick sounds like /k/ or /t/ short and quiet, with no extra "uh."

  • Slow-motion words. Say a word slowly and let your child guess it: "sssuuunnn" (sun), "mmmaaap" (map). Our guide to teaching blending explains how to say the sounds cleanly.
  • I spy a sound. "I spy something that starts with /s/."
  • Change one sound. "Say sat. Now change /s/ to /m/." (mat) Keep it to short words.
  • Rhyme chain. Take turns: snow, go, toe, no. Silly made-up words count too.
  • Clap the parts. Clap each part of a longer word: snow-man, win-ter, mit-ten, Grand-ma.

Choosing a new easy book: many bookstore and library "Level 1" readers aren't built around phonics and use lots of words a beginner can't sound out yet. Open to any page. If your child can sound out almost every word, it's a good fit.

What if travel or late nights break the routine?

Travel days, visitors and late nights will throw off the routine, and that's fine. Pick it up the next day with no catching up. A sound game in the car or spotting a known word on a sign still counts. Keep a couple of easy books in a bag so they come along on visits.

If your child pushes back, shrink it down to one page or one quick game. Let them choose the book and the place. Reading should feel like part of the holiday, not a test.

Questions parents ask

How many minutes a day should a kindergartener read over winter break?

About 10 minutes a day of your child reading or playing sound games is plenty for most kindergarteners, plus any time you spend reading aloud to them. Short and steady works better than long sessions. On busy days, one game or one page still counts, and it's fine to skip a day.

What should a kindergartener read over winter break?

Easy books your child can already read on their own, reread again and again, are the best choice for practice. Good options are the take-home books from school, decodable books that use the sounds your child knows, and their own writing. If your child is still learning letter sounds, practice a few sounds a day and play listening games instead. Add picture books that you read aloud for fun and new words.

What is a winter break reading bingo card?

A winter break reading bingo card is a grid of nine quick reading ideas, such as reading to a pet, spotting words your child knows on a sign, or writing a list. Your child colors a square each time they do one, and three in a row earns a small reward, like picking the bedtime story. It makes winter break reading feel like a game.

How do I keep my kindergartener reading during holiday travel?

Pack two easy books and play sound games that need no supplies, like guessing a word said slowly ("sssuuunnn" for sun) or "I spy" with first sounds (say "sss," not the letter name "ess"). Spotting short words your child knows on signs and gift tags counts as reading practice. If you miss a day, just pick up the next day and don't try to catch up.
