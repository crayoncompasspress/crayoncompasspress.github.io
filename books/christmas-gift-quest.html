What your child practices

  • Counting, matching and patterns

Try it first: download a free printable page (a stocking maze and a roll-a-snowman dice game (2 pages)).

A guide for grown-ups

A December activity book with one storyline from the first page to the last, so a few pages a day can feel like counting down to Christmas.

Use it as an activity countdown

The book is split into five stops on a map, from a snowy forest to Christmas Eve. One simple plan: start in early December and do about four or five pages a day. Your child colors a gift on the map after each stop, so they can see the quest moving toward Christmas Eve. Save the letter-to-Santa page for the night you write real letters.

Who it suits

It is made for ages 3 to 6. Three-year-olds will enjoy the coloring, tracing and simpler mazes with a grown-up nearby. Five- and six-year-olds can do most pages alone, including the dot-to-dots, patterns and spot-the-difference puzzles. Your child does not need to read: a grown-up reads the one-line instruction and the child does the rest.

Good for busy holiday days

  • Travel: one book and a few crayons cover a long car ride or a wait at the airport.
  • Holiday visits: a coloring page keeps a child busy in a quiet corner.
  • Classroom: teachers can use a maze, I Spy or dot-to-dot page as a winter-break warm-up.
  • Gift: it fits a stocking and pairs well with a new pack of crayons.

What children practice without noticing

Mazes and tracing build pencil control. Counting, matching and "what comes next" pages build early math and pattern skills. Draw-the-other-half and design-your-own pages leave room for creativity, so it is not all right-or-wrong answers.

Quick answers for grown-ups

How many pages a day should we do in December?

About four or five pages a day takes you from early December to Christmas Eve. Younger children can do two or three and skip the harder mazes.

Can a 3-year-old use it?

Yes, with a grown-up nearby. Coloring, tracing and the bigger mazes suit 3-year-olds; dot-to-dots and pattern pages suit ages 4 to 6.

Does my child need to read?

No. A grown-up reads the one-line instruction at the top of the page and the child does the activity.

Which crayons or pens work best?

Crayons and colored pencils. Markers can show through to the next page.

Is it a good stocking stuffer?

Yes. It is a screen-free book that keeps kids busy on travel days and holiday visits, and it pairs well with a new box of crayons.
