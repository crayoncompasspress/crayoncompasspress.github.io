About this book

Can you teach a robot to think? Only with math!

Leo and Ava find an old robot named Gearby in their school maker space. Gearby can follow rules, but it can't learn. Across 12 comic-book missions, kids use real 4th and 5th grade math to train Gearby, and they find out how artificial intelligence actually works along the way.

What's inside

  • 12 missions. Each one has a comic, a one-page Big Idea, 5 pages of practice and a Boss Puzzle.
  • About 460 practice problems: multiplication, division with remainders, fractions, decimals, rounding, place value, data and line plots, the coordinate plane, angles and measurement.
  • Real AI ideas, explained simply: rules vs. learning, data and labels, features, decision trees, nearest neighbors, pixels, neurons and weights, learning from mistakes, how chatbots predict the next word, unfair data, and spotting fake photos.
  • Logic puzzles that make kids think, including logic grids, a pixel-code puzzle and the "Who Am I?" puzzles.
  • A full answer key and a skills chart for grown-ups that lists the Common Core math skills each mission practices.
  • A Mission Map with badges to color, a glossary of "Lab Words", and a certificate at the end.

Why kids like it

  • It reads like a story, not a drill book.
  • It's middle-grade, not babyish: comics, a funny robot and real challenges.
  • No screen, app or chatbot is needed. Just a pencil.
  • Optional "Super Brain Bonus" boxes stretch kids who are ready for more.

It works well for homeschool, summer review, classroom enrichment or a rainy-day challenge for kids ages 9 – 11.

8.5 x 11 inches, 108 pages, black-and-white interior.

Quick answers for grown-ups

What grade is this math workbook for?

4th and 5th grade, about ages 9 to 11.

What math does it cover?

About 460 problems on multiplication, division with remainders, fractions, decimals, rounding, place value, data and line plots, the coordinate plane, angles and measurement.

Does my child need a computer or app?

No. Kids learn how AI works using only a pencil and paper.

Is there an answer key?

Yes, a full answer key plus a skills chart that lists the Common Core math skills in each mission.

Is there a companion book?

Yes: Robot Brain Puzzles (ages 8-12) continues the story with Leo, Ava and Gearby.
